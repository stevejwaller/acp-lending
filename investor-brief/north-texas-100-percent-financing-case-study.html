$17K out of pocket on an $889K fix-and-flip loan

By Steve Waller · Published November 24, 2026

← All case studies

Fix-and-Flip Case Study

ACP Real Estate Lending case study. A north Texas suburb flip where the loan covers 100% of the purchase and 100% of the renovation.

  • 100% of purchase and renovation financed
  • $889,000 total loan
  • About $17,400 cash at closing, before title, appraisal and insurance

The deal

A single-family house in a north Texas suburb. The investor has it under contract and plans a full gut renovation, including adding living area. The investor’s after-repair value is $1,250,000.

ItemAmount
Total project cost (purchase + renovation)$889,000
Investor’s after-repair value$1,250,000

The loan

ACP matched it to a 100% program. The loan funds the full purchase at closing and holds the full renovation budget for draws as the work gets done. At $889,000 it’s 71% of the after-repair value, under the program’s 75% cap. It’s interest-only, with a 9-month term and one 3-month extension available.

ItemAmount
Purchase funded at closing100%
Renovation held for draws100%
Total loan$889,000
Loan to after-repair value71.1%

Cash to close

With nothing going toward a down payment, the investor’s cash covers only the loan’s own costs, and the seller’s credit picks up part of those.

ItemAmount
Loan points (3%)$26,670
Lender fees and prepaid interest, less the seller credit-$9,252
Estimated cash at closing$17,418

Title, appraisal, inspection, insurance and property taxes are paid at closing on top of this, as on any purchase. Figures are estimates on terms quoted this week and are subject to underwriting and appraisal.

Why it matters to investors

On a typical 90% program this same project would need roughly $90,000 to $100,000 at the table. Here it’s about $17,000. That’s $70,000 or more the investor keeps for the next deal, for overruns, or for carrying costs.

Two things still have to be true. The loan’s capped at 75% of the after-repair value, so the appraisal has to come in around $1,185,000 or higher for the 100% to hold. And the lender still wants to see about $94,500 in liquid funds. You don’t spend it, but you have to show it.

100% financing doesn’t make a deal good. It makes a good deal cheaper to get into. Run your ARV against closed sales in the subdivision before you sign the contract, not after you’ve got a loan.
