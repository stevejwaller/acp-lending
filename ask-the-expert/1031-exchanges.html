Ask the Expert: Five Common 1031 Exchange Mistakes

A 1031 exchange is one of the most powerful tax tools available to real estate investors—but it's also one of the easiest to mess up. We asked Theresa Quartaro, a Qualified Intermediary with IPX1031, the five mistakes she sees most often and what savvy investors should know before they list their next property.

1. Not Understanding the 45-Day Identification Window

The biggest trap? Investors think the 180-day window is more forgiving than it actually is. The truth is simple: those 180 days break into two hard calendar deadlines, and there's no flexibility on either one.

"Investors don't realize that the 180 days—45 to identify and 135 after to close—are hard calendar days, with very strict timelines," Theresa says. The good news is you can get creative. You can identify your replacement property and even place it under contract before you close on the sale. Once your property closes (the relinquished property), you're free to close on your replacement. Timing it this way keeps you in control. And here's what matters: identifying your target before you sell doesn't negate the exchange, as long as the rest of the mechanics line up.

2. Missing the 180-Day Deadline

The 180-day deadline is hard stop. There's no extension, no exception, no mulligan.

"If an exchanger misses any deadline, the exchange fails," Theresa explains. "We send two notices before day 45 and another reminder at day 180. If they fail to close on the replacement property, there are no extensions. The exchange fails, we send their funds back the next business day after day 180, and they report it as a sale, not an exchange—taxes due when they file." The difference in tax bill? Sometimes six figures. Don't let it happen to you.

3. Bringing in the QI Too Late

Timing is everything. And the sooner you involve your Qualified Intermediary, the better.

"The sooner the better, especially if there's a partnership or LLC involved," Theresa says. "There's a lot to discuss and many questions to answer so the exchanger understands the process and requirements." Here's the killer mistake: waiting until the sale is closing. "If the sale takes place and no exchange is set up, it's too late afterward. We can normally set up the exchange within a few days of closing, but the sooner the better to avoid any partnership issues if the property is in a partnership or LLC."

Bottom line: contact your QI early. If there's an entity involved, contact them even earlier.

4. Not Lining Up Financing in Time

Your lender needs to be in the loop, and your timing needs to be locked down before you sell.

Make sure your lender understands the timeline. Your financing can't sit in limbo while the 180 days tick away. Get everything lined up so your closing happens inside that window.

5. Not Understanding What You're Actually Doing

Here's Theresa's final piece of advice, and it might be the most important: "Make sure that they understand what the 1031 is utilized for—selling investment property. Make sure they address any and all questions or concerns." Don't assume you know how it works. Ask. Ask again. Make sure every corner of your deal is covered before you sell.

The 1031 is a powerful tool. Make sure you're using it correctly.
